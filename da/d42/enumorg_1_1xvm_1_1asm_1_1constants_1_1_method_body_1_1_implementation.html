Detailed Description

An enumeration of various forms of method body implementations.

  • Implicit - the method body represents a method known to exist for compilation purposes, but is otherwise not present; this is the result of the into clause, or the methods of Object in the context of an interface, for example;
  • Declared - the method body represents a declared but non-implemented method;
  • Default - the method body is a default implementation from an interface;
  • Abstract - the method body is on a class, but is explicitly abstract;
  • SansCode - the method body has no code, but isn't explicitly abstract;
  • Capped - the method body represents the "cap" on a method chain;
  • Delegating - the method body is implemented by delegating to the same signature on a different reference;
  • Field - the method body represents access to a property's underlying field, which occurs when a property's method is overridden and calls super();
  • Native - the method body is implemented natively by the runtime;
  • Explicit - the method body is represented by byte code that gets executed.
